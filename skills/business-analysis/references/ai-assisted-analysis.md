# AI-assisted analysis

Use for requirements synthesized from interviews, records, documents, or several conflicting sources. A small explicit request usually needs only its acceptance condition.

## Preserve the evidence

Keep important claims traceable to the supplied source and its status: accepted decision, observation, proposal, or assumption. Record material omissions, inaccessible attachments, truncated transcripts, stale versions, and missing stakeholder perspectives. Do not call the analysis complete merely because the available text has been summarized.

Check proposed rules against their context. A question, joke, option, or tentative discussion is not a decision. Preserve dissent and identify who can resolve it. Role-play and synthetic personas can generate questions to investigate; they cannot confirm real needs or consent. Protect confidential source material within the task's authorized tools and destinations.

## Produce a useful decision

For a substantial change, connect the proposed requirement to the problem it addresses. Compare plausible options, including a simpler process or no change when relevant; do not assume an AI solution is the objective. Identify which missing evidence would change the recommendation. Bring material tradeoffs to the existing decision owner without requiring a new approval ceremony for settled decisions.

Use a compact outcome statement when useful:

> We expect [accepted change] to improve [observable business outcome] for [actors]. We will compare [measure and baseline] over [agreed period]. [Known owner] interprets the result. Unknown values remain pending.

This is an optional structure, not a demand for numerical targets on every task. A completed specification, generated story count, and response speed are delivery outputs. Less rework, fewer incorrect decisions, or improved task completion are possible outcomes that require measurement. Do not invent targets, assign owners without evidence, or claim improvement from a before/after anecdote with different scope.

## Hand off what is known

Keep accepted rules and unresolved decisions visible to design, implementation, and review. Give the material assumption, its impact, and the next source or decision needed. Mark generated examples as proposals until they derive from accepted rules. A reviewer agreeing with an AI-generated interpretation is a consistency check, not new stakeholder evidence.
