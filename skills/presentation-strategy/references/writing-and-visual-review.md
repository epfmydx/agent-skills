# Writing and visual review

Review usefulness and meaning. AI assistance does not by itself establish poor quality, and avoiding a list of recognizable words does not establish good quality.

## Substance before surface

For each material statement, ask what it tells this audience and what supports it. Generic benefits, invented certainty, missing comparisons, and an unclear decision cannot be repaired by changing a font or adding an image.

Preserve truth while editing:

- Keep the relevant population, time window, unit, baseline, and uncertainty. A percentage without its denominator or comparison may mislead even when the number is correct.
- Retain distinctions among a target, estimate, forecast, proposal, pilot, and observed result. Do not convert a staged demo into proof of production readiness.
- Separate source claims from the presenter's inference. A source citation does not support a stronger conclusion than the source actually makes.
- When evidence is missing, ask for it if necessary, omit the unsupported claim, or identify an assumption or gap clearly. Do not invent precise data to make a chart feel complete.

## Natural, specific wording

Prefer words that identify the actual subject, action, or consequence. Use necessary technical terms consistently and explain them when the audience needs it. Do not replace ordinary language with inflated abstractions or strip out meaningful jargon just to sound simple.

These are illustrative rewrites, not claims about a real product or project:

| Weak wording | Why it is weak | More useful direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unlock seamless collaboration | No named capability or outcome | Each task has an owner, deadline, and shared discussion |
| Drive transformative operational excellence | No observable change | Reduce the steps required to approve a purchase |
| Strong momentum across key initiatives | No identifiable progress | Name the milestones completed and the work that slipped |
| AI-powered insights | The mechanism and value are unclear | State what the system identifies, from which inputs, and how someone uses it |
| A 30% improvement | Measure and comparison are missing | Name the metric, baseline, population, and period from the actual evidence |

Do not invent details while applying these examples. If the source says only that collaboration improved, the rewrite must not assert an unverified task-management feature.

Watch for repeated slogans, identical sentence rhythms, unnecessary three-part lists, ornate synonyms, awkward compounds, and filler subtitles. Keep any wording that earns its place. A punctuation mark, passive sentence, or common word is not sufficient evidence that a passage is poor or AI-generated. Follow the requested voice and applicable authoring instructions while judging the larger meaning.

Read the text aloud when useful. It should sound like something the presenter can explain naturally. Keep official names, necessary legal or technical wording, quotations, and distinctions intact.

## Titles and structure

Choose the form that fits the slide:

- **Finding:** a supported takeaway, such as a measured comparison with its actual context.
- **Explanation or setup:** a direct subject title, such as "Approval process" or "Pilot assumptions".
- **Decision:** a clear description of the decision or recommendation, with certainty that matches the evidence.

Do not make every title a slogan, a question, or an insight claim. A simple topic label can be useful. Conversely, a vague label such as "Results" may hide the point when a slide actually establishes a specific finding.

Read the sequence without body text to identify repetition or gaps. Then read titles and body together to catch claims that the evidence does not establish. Do not force a continuous title-only story at the expense of clear setup slides.

## Visual choices

Choose the visual that makes the relationship easier to understand:

| Content need | Useful visual | Check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Comparison or change measured in data | Native chart or readable table | Units, dates, scales, denominators, uncertainty, and source |
| Mechanism, architecture, or process | Diagram | Correct relationships, direction, labels, and relevant exceptions |
| Product behavior | Actual screenshot, demo, or clearly labeled mockup | Fidelity, sensitive information, viewing size, and status of the depicted behavior |
| Human or physical context | Relevant photograph or illustration | Authenticity, rights, cultural fit, and whether it is mistaken for evidence |
| Conceptual framing | Purposeful typography or a useful metaphor | Whether it clarifies the concept or merely decorates the page |

Use generated imagery for appropriate illustrative or decorative purposes. Do not use it to fabricate customers, evidence, measured charts, or a working interface. Retain native editable objects when the task requires them and use the authoring workflow's rules for creating visuals.

## Avoid interchangeable layouts

Start from the requested brand or template. Otherwise establish a coherent hierarchy, type system, palette, spacing, and image treatment. Vary composition when the content calls for it; do not vary it just to make every slide different.

Review repeated equal-size cards, generic icons, unsupported decorative charts, stock business imagery, and fashionable gradients when they weaken hierarchy or obscure the actual content. These are contextual design concerns, not banned ingredients. A comparison may legitimately need columns, and a dashboard presentation may legitimately show a dashboard.

Empty space can improve focus, but do not remove essential evidence to achieve minimalism. Cut duplication or move supporting detail appropriately before reducing type until it becomes unreadable. Use the template and intended viewing conditions to judge scale rather than applying a single font-size rule to every format.

## Accessibility and delivery checks

Check sufficient contrast, reading order, meaningful titles, useful alternative text, and captions where relevant. Label distinctions as well as coloring them. Avoid motion-dependent meaning and unnecessary animation; provide a stable way to understand essential content.

Render and inspect the final slides when authoring tools permit. Look for clipped text, overlaps, unreadable labels, poor crops, low-resolution evidence, inconsistent hierarchy, and changed meaning after export. Inspect requested editability separately. A screenshot proves appearance at that size, not screen-reader accessibility or editable chart data.

For live delivery, speaker notes should add helpful context or talking points. Estimate timing against the actual content and interaction. Prepare likely objections and questions when useful, while acknowledging answers that need further evidence. Do not claim a rehearsal or application check happened unless it did.

## What successful review establishes

Record concrete defects and fixes rather than a generic quality score. A convincing-looking deck can still fail on accuracy or audience fit. Separate structural checks, model review, application tests, and human audience feedback. One synthetic scenario is a smoke check, not a measured reliability or persuasion result.
